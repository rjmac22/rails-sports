## Analytical spine from Study 21

What does it mean to win?
→ runs are the basic score
→ runs are accumulated within constraints
→ wickets, deliveries and time matter
→ their value changes with match state
→ tactics influence probabilities rather than determine outcomes
→ the fielding side faces trade-offs between wickets and run prevention
→ field placement is one way of managing those trade-offs
→ a field setting is a spatial allocation of limited fielding resources

## Reporting rule

Do not collect cricket material simply because it is interesting. Every source, example or quote should either:

- establish a fact or rule;
- make one part of the analytical spine visible in a real match;
- test or challenge our interpretation;
- show how players, captains, coaches or commentators describe the same problem in cricket language;
- help a general reader understand why the trade-off matters.

## Core reporting questions

### Formal foundations

- How do the Laws formally define winning and scoring?
- How do Test, ODI and T20 playing conditions constrain innings differently?
- Which rules establish the limited fielding resources available to a captain?

### Match-state value

- Can we find situations where a run, wicket, delivery or minute has obviously different value because of match state?
- Where does a side stop pursuing one objective and begin protecting another?
- Can the same event be good for one side and bad for the other for reasons that are not visible in the raw score alone?

### Wickets versus run prevention

- What do captains mean when they describe a field as attacking or defensive?
- When will a captain deliberately concede runs to increase wicket-taking chances?
- When will a captain reduce wicket-taking opportunities in order to suppress scoring?
- Does preventing runs sometimes create wicket opportunities indirectly?

### Field placement

- What is actually being changed when a fielder moves?
- Is "spatial allocation of limited fielding resources" a fair description from a practitioner’s point of view?
- What important cricket knowledge would that analytical description miss if left on its own?

## Editorial cautions

- Do not present familiar cricket knowledge as a new discovery.
- Do not turn the notebook into prose paragraph by paragraph.
- Do not overload the piece with examples; use only those that perform a clear explanatory job.
- Do not let technical terminology replace cricket language where ordinary cricket language is clearer.
- Distinguish what the Laws establish from what our analytical interpretation adds.
- Treat the framework as something to test against cricket practice, not something cricket must conform to.
