# Cricket Glossary

A shared reference for cricket terminology used across the standalone cricket studies and journalism pieces.

This is not intended to be a complete cricket dictionary. Add terms when they become relevant to the work.

## Ball / delivery

A single act of the bowler delivering the ball to the striker.

In ordinary usage, "ball" and "delivery" are often used interchangeably, although some deliveries such as wides and no-balls do not count as one of the six legal deliveries in an over.

## Over

A sequence of six legal deliveries bowled by the same bowler from one end of the pitch.

After the over finishes, play continues from the opposite end with a different bowler. Because the batters do not automatically change ends between overs, this matters for which batter is on strike at the start of the next over.

## Striker

The batter facing the current delivery.

## Non-striker

The other batter, standing at the bowler's end while the striker faces the delivery.

## Strike

The position of being the batter who faces the next delivery.

To be "on strike" means you will face the next ball.

## Retain the strike

To arrange the scoring so that the same batter will face the first ball of the next over.

For example, if the striker scores one run from the final ball of an over, the batters change ends because of the run. The next over is then bowled from the opposite end, which means that same batter is again on strike.

## Farm the strike / farming the strike

A batting tactic in which the stronger batter deliberately tries to face most of the deliveries, usually when batting with a much weaker partner or tailender.

This can involve refusing singles early in an over, preferring even numbers of runs such as two, four or six, and taking a single late in the over so the stronger batter retains the strike for the next over.

## Tailender

A batter from the lower part of the batting order, typically selected primarily for bowling rather than batting and therefore usually expected to be a weaker batter than those higher in the order.

## No. 11

The final player in a team's batting order.

Because a cricket innings normally ends when ten wickets have fallen, the No. 11 is usually the last batter to come in. If the No. 11 is dismissed, the innings is over.

## Wicket

The term has several meanings in cricket. In match-state discussion, "a wicket" usually means the dismissal of a batter.

A batting side normally has ten wickets available in an innings: once ten players have been dismissed, there is no remaining partner for the eleventh batter and the innings ends.

## Boundary

Either the edge of the playing field or a scoring shot that reaches or clears it.

A ball reaching the boundary after touching the ground normally scores four runs. A ball clearing the boundary without bouncing normally scores six.

## Four

Four runs scored when the ball reaches the boundary after touching the ground, subject to the Laws and any relevant extras.

## Six

Six runs scored when the striker hits the ball and it clears the boundary without first touching the ground.

## Wicket maiden

An over in which the bowler concedes no runs from the bat or extras charged to the bowler and also takes a wicket.

## Sweeper / boundary rider

A fielder positioned relatively deep, often close to the boundary, primarily to intercept powerful shots and reduce the chance of four or six runs.

"Sweeper" is informal positional language rather than one fixed named fielding position.

## Close catcher

A fielder positioned close to the batter in a place where a mishit or edged shot may produce a catch.

Slip fielders are common examples.

## Slip

A close-catching fielding position behind the batter on the off side, commonly used to catch edges from the bat.

## LBW

Short for "leg before wicket".

A batter can be dismissed lbw when a legal delivery hits the batter's body and, subject to the detailed conditions in the Laws, would otherwise have gone on to hit the wicket.

## Review / DRS

A review is a team's request for the on-field umpire's decision to be checked using the Decision Review System (DRS), where available.

Teams have a limited number of unsuccessful reviews, so whether a review remains available can itself affect a later match situation.

## Innings

A period in which one team bats and the other fields.

The way an innings ends depends on the format and match state: for example, ten wickets may fall, an overs limit may be reached, the batting captain may declare in Test cricket, or a target may be achieved.

## Declaration

In Test and other multi-innings cricket, the batting captain can voluntarily end the team's innings before all ten wickets have fallen.

This is usually done because the captain believes the value of having more time to dismiss the opposition exceeds the value of continuing to accumulate runs.

## Draw

A possible result in Test and other time-limited multi-innings cricket when the match ends without either side satisfying the conditions for victory.

A draw is different from a tie.

## Tie

A result in which the scores are level when the match has otherwise reached the conditions required for completion under the relevant Laws or playing conditions.

A tie and a draw are not the same result.
