# Rails Sports article game plan

A repeatable workflow for taking an idea from question to published article without mixing research, writing, editing and presentation.

The governing principle remains:

> **The simulation is the engine. The sporting question is the product.**

---

## Stage 1 — Find the question

Start with a real sporting question, not a statistical technique.

Ask:

- What is the actual question?
- Who in sport would care?
- Where is the disagreement, uncertainty or surprise?
- What decision, constraint or misconception might the analysis change?
- **Where’s the consequence?**

If there is no consequence, put the idea in the ideas file rather than forcing an article.

### Output

A one- or two-sentence story question.

---

## Stage 2 — Build the evidence

Do only the research needed to answer the question credibly.

Create or extend:

- notebook(s);
- raw data;
- calculations/models;
- source ledger;
- reporting notes;
- primary-source material where practical.

Separate:

- observed facts;
- model outputs;
- participant recollections;
- journalistic interpretation.

Record limitations immediately rather than relying on memory.

### Stop rule

Do not keep researching because more information exists.

Research further only when:

- a central claim is unsupported;
- competing explanations need testing;
- uncertainty materially affects the conclusion;
- the story requires a missing piece.

---

## Stage 3 — Find the story

Before drafting, decide what the reader is being shown.

For each possible example or passage ask:

> What does this reveal that the reader would not otherwise see?

Prefer examples where the same apparently simple statistic or action changes meaning because the underlying constraint changes.

Build the article around consequences, not a chronological dump of research.

### Output

A short structure / story spine.

---

## Stage 4 — Draft

Write the article for a reader, not for the notebook.

Use:

- concrete situations;
- only the technical detail needed to understand the consequence;
- specific details that add meaning or texture;
- plain explanations of unfamiliar cricket/statistical terms.

Avoid:

- narrating things a viewer can already see;
- method sections disguised as prose;
- facts included simply because they were researched;
- commentary with no analytical purpose.

Do not worry yet about standfirsts, SEO, pull quotes or page furniture.

### Output

Complete rough article.

---

## Stage 5 — Edit the writing

Now apply the writing tools.

Check:

- strong subjects and verbs;
- sentence length and rhythm;
- unnecessary words;
- repetition;
- showing versus telling;
- transitions;
- clichés;
- first-order creativity;
- whether emphasis is earned;
- whether technical explanation arrives when needed.

### First-order creativity check

Flag:

- obvious puns;
- stock metaphors;
- first associations;
- familiar sports-writing phrases;
- the first “clever” line most writers would also reach for.

Generate another level of thought before settling.

### Lock rule

When a passage has been deliberately reviewed and agreed, mark it mentally as locked.

Do not reopen it in later passes without a specific reason.

---

## Stage 6 — Fact-check the final draft

Check the **actual edited prose**, line by line.

Verify:

- scores and dates;
- player roles;
- quotations;
- injuries;
- rules;
- match states;
- chronology;
- market figures;
- calculations;
- tactical claims attributed to players/coaches;
- physical/visual details;
- exact wording of who decided or said what.

Prefer primary or contemporary sources for central claims where available.

### Output

Every factual claim either supported, qualified or removed.

---

## Stage 7 — Prose–evidence alignment

This is a distinct pass.

Ask of every precise detail:

> Does the source support this exact wording and level of precision?

Look especially for:

- plausible but unsourced visual details;
- compressed attribution;
- overprecise timing;
- causal language stronger than the evidence;
- retrospective recollection presented as contemporaneous fact;
- model estimates presented as exact observations.

This is where “strapped up” versus “padded up” type errors should be caught.

### Output

Final evidence-safe prose.

---

## Stage 8 — Final proof

Now stop editing the article substantively.

Check only:

- spelling;
- punctuation;
- missing/repeated words;
- names;
- heading hierarchy;
- Markdown;
- broken links;
- formatting consistency.

If a genuine factual or structural problem appears, fix it. Otherwise leave the prose alone.

### Output

Clean final text.

---

## Stage 9 — Presentation/editorial layer

Package the finished article for publication.

Make one decision at a time:

1. headline;
2. standfirst/deck — if useful;
3. byline/date;
4. hero image;
5. captions/credits;
6. charts or diagrams;
7. subhead treatment;
8. pull quotes — only if they improve scanning/layout;
9. page rhythm and spacing;
10. Notes and sources;
11. SEO title and description;
12. social preview / share text.

Do not assume every article needs every element.

### Source style

Default Rails Sports approach:

- clean article body;
- no citation clutter unless a link genuinely benefits the reader;
- short **Notes and sources** section;
- link to notebooks/reporting files for the audit trail.

---

## Stage 10 — Publication check

Before publishing ask:

- Does the headline accurately promise what the article delivers?
- Does the opening make the question clear?
- Does every section advance the argument?
- Can a non-expert follow the necessary terms?
- Are the main numbers/quotes/rules auditable?
- Are visuals doing explanatory work rather than decoration?
- Does the ending complete this story rather than tease work that should have been included?
- Is the page readable on mobile?
- Is anything obviously unfinished?

If yes, publish.

Do not invent another editing pass.

---

## Stage 11 — Post-publication note

After publication, record only useful lessons:

- reader confusion;
- factual correction;
- layout problem;
- strong/weak distribution channel;
- question the article generated;
- reusable research or code;
- follow-up story.

Do not retrospectively rewrite the article endlessly.

---

# Fast checklist

For later articles, the workflow reduces to:

1. **Question** — what matters?
2. **Evidence** — what can we establish?
3. **Story** — where is the consequence?
4. **Draft** — explain it.
5. **Edit** — make it read well.
6. **Fact-check** — verify the final prose.
7. **Alignment** — remove unsupported precision.
8. **Proof** — mechanics only.
9. **Present** — package it.
10. **Publish** — ship.

Each stage has a job. Do not make every stage do every job.
