## Agreed opening

Football was invented in 1863.

Before then, people across Britain played all sorts of games they called football. Different schools and clubs had different rules, and nobody could agree on how the game should be played.

Then the Football Association came along, established a common set of rules, and the beautiful game was born.

Or so the story goes.

People had been playing games called football for centuries. Medieval versions varied from place to place, but they weren't entirely without rules. Players followed local customs, even if those rules weren't written down.

By the nineteenth century, Britain's public schools had developed their own versions of football. At Rugby, boys could run with the ball. At Eton, they played games that relied much more on kicking, while Harrow and Winchester had their own rules and traditions. These weren't incomplete versions of a game waiting to be invented. They were established games that worked perfectly well for the boys playing them.
