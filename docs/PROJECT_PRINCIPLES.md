# Project principles

Inside Rails starts with sporting questions, not statistical techniques.

Every article should follow this sequence:

1. **Start with a genuine sporting debate.**
   Choose a question that supporters, players, coaches or managers might naturally argue about. The sporting decision is the subject; the model is the tool.

2. **Build a rigorous model.**
   Represent the real decision as faithfully as the available evidence allows. State assumptions clearly, validate the implementation and separate measured inputs from illustrative assumptions.

3. **Test competing ideas.**
   Compare recognisable strategies, conventional wisdom and plausible alternative interpretations rather than constructing a model that can only confirm one preferred answer.

4. **Show the evidence.**
   Preserve reproducible code, tests, tables and figures. Report uncertainty, sensitivity and limitations rather than presenting a simulated result as unquestionable fact.

5. **Explain what it means in plain English.**
   Translate the result back into the sporting decision. Readers should understand what the evidence changes, what it does not establish and where reasonable disagreement remains.

## Working rule

**The simulation is the engine. The sporting question is the product.**

A technically impressive model is not sufficient by itself. The work should answer a question that matters outside the notebook and should not require the reader to care about the mathematical machinery before they care about the result.
